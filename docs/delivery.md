# Delivery and handover

Describe how the project's result will reach the people it serves. This might
mean handing over a report, holding an event, introducing a process, publishing
creative work or releasing a product.

## Use and handover

Explain what people receive, how they use it, any prerequisites and who takes
responsibility afterward. Link to relevant materials, instructions, access
arrangements and support contacts.

## Delivery acceptance and remaining work

Connect the [project expectations](../PROJECT.md#scope-and-expectations) to the
relevant [checks](../tests/README.md) and [evidence](../evidence/README.md). State
separately what the evidence establishes about delivery acceptance, outcome
success and execution constraints, and what remains unproven. Record known
limitations and any acceptance decision. Keep prepared, created, checked,
delivered and accepted states clear when they differ.

Record closure against the agreed completion boundary, or an authorized
decision to stop early. Settle or transfer remaining obligations and clean up
temporary resources where relevant.

## Outcomes and learning

Assess outcome success against the intended benefit. Delivery acceptance alone
does not establish that the benefit occurred. If assessment needs later use,
assign an owner and a time or condition for the check, and identify who will act
on its findings. Have the receiving owner explicitly accept these
responsibilities and any pending checks at handover. Set a review date for a
pending check if its triggering condition may not occur.

Retain useful findings from actual use and connect follow-up work to
[current-work.md](current-work.md). Use existing records where they already
capture the information, and keep useful findings together without duplicating
them in a separate report.
